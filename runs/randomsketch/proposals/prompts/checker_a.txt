Check three things, and only these:
1. Content. What `title` and `content` must say depends on the entry's `path`:
   - `move` (a proved result that moves as is): exactly what the Lean statement (`lean_type`) proves — the same hypotheses and the same conclusion, neither weaker nor stronger, every object named correctly. Read the Lean, not the words.
   - `generalize` with a `lean_type` (a proved result rebuilt in general form): a general statement of which `lean_type` is a direct special case, free of the paper's own notation and setting (`paper_shaped` lists what ties the Lean to the paper; none of it may appear in the content). `dropped_hypotheses` lists hypotheses the paper's proof never uses: the content leaves them out, and it must still hold without them.
   - `generalize` without a `lean_type` (a cited result to be built): what the cited work states (`source_text` when present, else what `reference` and `content` say the cited work states), in its own generality, no weaker and no stronger.
   `relation_to_paper` is a separate remark on how the paper's result relates; judge `content` alone.
   `content_matches` is false only when the content fails this (for `generalize`: the general statement is false, or the paper's result does not follow from it); the note says how. A wording or placement objection leaves `content_matches` true.
2. Duplication. Using `search_hits`, `nearest_existing` and the existing toolkit modules: is the result already available (up to notation, argument order or trivially weaker hypotheses) in a base library or the toolkit? If so, `duplicate_of` is that declaration's full name; otherwise null.
